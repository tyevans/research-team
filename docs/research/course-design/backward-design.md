# Backward Design / Understanding by Design (UbD)

**Scope:** how the Wiggins & McTighe backward-design workflow actually operates in practice — the artifacts produced, the decision sequence, the review gates, and where it is iterative rather than linear. Final section maps the process onto a mechanizable multi-agent pipeline.

## 0. What UbD is and is not

UbD is **a planning process plus a set of design tools plus a set of design standards** — explicitly *not* a prescriptive recipe or a program. The *Nutshell* states this as tenet 1: "It offers a 3-stage design process, a set of helpful design tools, and design standards -- not a rigid program or prescriptive recipe."

Two ideas carry the whole framework (ASCD white paper, intro): (1) teach and assess for **understanding and transfer**, and (2) design **backward** from those ends.

"Backward" has two senses (white paper, FAQ 1):

1. Plan with the end in mind — results (Stage 1) → evidence (Stage 2) → means (Stage 3).
2. It is backward relative to how most people plan, who start by listing activities (Stage 3) with only a vague sense of results and almost no attention to evidence.

The unit — not the lesson — is the design object. The white paper (FAQ 3) is explicit: understandings, essential questions, and transfer performance tasks "are too complex and multifaceted to be satisfactorily addressed within a single lesson." Lessons are planned *inside* the frame the unit establishes.

The two failure modes UbD is designed to prevent are named the **twin sins**:

- **Activity-oriented design** — hands-on without minds-on; students engage in the activity without being challenged to extract ideas or connections.
- **Coverage-oriented design** — marching through a textbook or standards list; the textbook is treated as the curriculum rather than as a resource.

Both are described in the white paper as symptoms of "no clear priorities and purposes."

## 1. Stage 1 — Desired Results

### Driving questions

From the *Nutshell*:

- What long-term **transfer goals** are targeted?
- What **meanings** should students make?
- What **essential questions** will students explore?
- What **knowledge & skill** will students acquire?

From the white paper: "What should students know, understand, and be able to do? What is the ultimate transfer we seek as a result of this unit? What enduring understandings are desired? What essential questions will be explored in-depth and provide focus to all learning?"

### Inputs

Established goals and external requirements: content standards (national/state/province/district), program- or mission-related goals, course or program objectives, accreditation outcomes, habits of mind and cross-disciplinary goals (Template 2.0 field prompt: "What content standards and program- or mission-related goal(s) will this unit address? What habits of mind and cross-disciplinary goal(s) — for example, 21st century skills, core competencies — will this unit address?").

Also: the discipline's source material — texts, primary sources, data sets, professional practice, the instructor's or SME's own expertise. This is where **unstructured source material first enters** (see §5).

The white paper is blunt about the constraint that forces the work: "Because there is typically more content than can reasonably be addressed within the available time, teachers are obliged to make choices. This first stage in the design process calls for clarity about priorities." Stage 1 is fundamentally a **prioritization** step, not a listing step.

### Artifacts produced

Template 2.0 organizes Stage 1 into three tiers — **Transfer**, **Meaning**, **Acquisition** — with **Established Goals** as a left-hand column spanning all three.

| Artifact | Definition (from *Nutshell*) | Canonical stem |
|---|---|---|
| **Established Goals** | External requirements the unit answers to | (verbatim standard / outcome text) |
| **Transfer Goal(s)** | "States the long-term accomplishments that students should be able to *do* with knowledge and skill, on their own." Frames standards as long-term performance accomplishments. Answers "Why?" and "What can you do with this?" | "Students will be able to independently use their learning to …" |
| **Understandings** (enduring understandings) | A student-constructed understanding about a big idea — the "moral of the story." Connects otherwise discrete facts; transferable; usually not obvious and may be counter-intuitive; cannot be transmitted, must be earned; is an inference stated as a **specific generalization** | "Students will understand that …" |
| **Essential Questions** | Open-ended, no simple right answer; meant to be investigated and argued; provoke meaning-making; raise further questions; arise naturally in life and in "doing" the subject; recur fruitfully over time | "Students will keep considering …" |
| **Knowledge** | Facts and basic concepts to recall — both *targeted* and *enabling* | "Students will know …" |
| **Skill** | Discrete skills and processes | "Students will be skilled at …" |
| **Predicted misunderstandings** | Template 1.0 asks directly: "What misunderstandings are predictable?" *Nutshell* Stage 1 note 6: "We must be mindful of potential student **mis**understandings and transfer deficits." In Template 2.0 this migrates into Stage 3's Progress Monitoring column ("What are potential rough spots and student misunderstandings?") | (list) |

Sample transfer goals from the white paper, Figure 1 — note they are discipline-level and long-horizon, not unit-level:

- Mathematics: "Apply mathematical knowledge, skill, and reasoning to solve real-world problems."
- Writing: "Effectively write for various audiences to explain (narrative, expository), entertain (creative), persuade (persuasive), and help others perform a task (technical)."
- History: "Apply lessons of the past (historical patterns) to current and future events and issues. Critically appraise historical claims."

Sample understanding/EQ pairs (white paper, Figure 2):

- U: "Great literature explores universal themes of human existence and can reveal truths through fiction." → EQ: "How can stories from other places and times relate to our current lives?"
- U: "The geography, climate, and natural resources of a region influence the culture, economy, and lifestyle of its inhabitants." → EQ: "How does where we live influence how we live?"

### The key scoping rule

*Nutshell*, Stage 1 note 7 — this is the single most operational constraint in the whole stage:

> "Resist listing all the possible knowledge and skill goals that are in any way related to the unit topic. Identify **only** those goals that you plan to directly assess in STAGE 2 and explicitly address in STAGE 3."

This is a forward reference from Stage 1 into Stages 2–3 and is the reason the process cannot be executed as a strict one-pass pipeline (see §6).

Note also *Nutshell* Stage 1 note 5: transfer goals must be stated explicitly **even when the established standards do not name them**. The designer is expected to infer and author content that the input documents do not contain.

### Quality gate — UbD Design Standards, Stage 1

"To what extent does the design **focus on the 'big ideas' of targeted content**?" Consider — are:

- the targeted understandings **enduring**, based on transferable big ideas at the heart of the discipline, and **in need of "uncoverage"**?
- the targeted understandings framed as **specific generalizations**?
- the big ideas framed by questions that spark meaningful connections, provoke genuine inquiry and deep thought, and encourage transfer?
- appropriate goals (content standards, benchmarks, curriculum objectives) identified?
- valid and unit-relevant knowledge and skills identified?

### Stage 1 failure modes

- Understandings written as topics ("the water cycle") or as truisms rather than as contestable, specific generalizations. The standard's phrase "in need of 'uncoverage'" is the discriminator: if it is obvious, it is not an enduring understanding.
- Essential questions that are actually comprehension questions with a right answer, or that are so broad they attach to no content.
- Transfer goals that restate the unit content instead of naming an autonomous, long-horizon accomplishment.
- Unbounded knowledge/skill lists (violates note 7 above).
- Standards copied in wholesale without unpacking. Tenet 3 of the *Nutshell*: UbD "'unpacks' and transforms Content Standards into the relevant Stage 1 elements and appropriate assessments in Stage 2." Unpacking is a required transformation, not a copy.

## 2. Stage 2 — Evidence

### Driving questions

*Nutshell*:

- What performances and products will reveal evidence of **meaning-making** and **transfer**?
- What additional evidence will be collected for other Desired Results?

White paper: "How will we know if students have achieved the desired results? What will we accept as evidence of student understanding and their ability to use (transfer) their learning in new situations? How will we evaluate student performance in fair and consistent ways?"

The governing instruction is to **"think like an assessor before designing specific units and lessons"** (white paper, Stage 2). The focus is *valid evidence*: "making sure that what we assess and how we assess follows logically from the STAGE 1 goals" (*Nutshell*, Stage 2 note 1).

### Inputs

The full Stage 1 artifact set, plus the **Six Facets of Understanding**, which act as a generator/selector for task types.

### The Six Facets (task-generation tool)

From the *Nutshell* and white paper — six equal, non-hierarchical indicators (explicitly *not* a Bloom-style hierarchy; white paper FAQ 4):

| Facet | Student behavior |
|---|---|
| **Explain** | Generalizes, makes connections, has a sound theory, puts it in own words, teaches it, justifies, shows reasoning |
| **Interpret** | Offers a plausible, supported account of text/data/experience; uses images, analogies, stories, models |
| **Apply** | Transfers, adapts, adjusts; addresses novel issues and problems in new and complex contexts |
| **Perspective** | Sees the big picture; recognizes different points of view |
| **Empathy** | Perceives sensitively; walks in another's shoes |
| **Self-knowledge** | Metacognitive awareness; sees the limits of their own understanding; reflects |

Selection rule (white paper, FAQ 5 and Stage 2 point 1): use **only** the facets that give appropriate evidence of the targeted understanding. Math tends toward apply/interpret/explain; social studies adds empathy and perspective. Using all six is an anti-pattern.

Scope rule (white paper, Stage 2 point 2): facet-based performance tasks "are not intended for use in daily lessons. Rather, these tasks should be seen as culminating performances for a unit of study."

### Artifacts produced

**Template 2.0, Stage 2 — Evidence** has three columns: **Code** | **Evaluative Criteria** | *(evidence body)*.

| Artifact | Prompt / content |
|---|---|
| **Performance Task(s)** | "Students will show that they really understand by evidence of …"; "How will students demonstrate their understanding (meaning-making and transfer) through complex performance?" |
| **Other Evidence** | "Students will show they have achieved Stage 1 goals by …"; "What other evidence will you collect to determine whether Stage 1 goals were achieved?" — quizzes, tests, academic prompts, observations, homework, journals, work samples |
| **Evaluative Criteria** | "What criteria will be used in each assessment to evaluate attainment of the desired results?" / "Regardless of the format of the assessment, what qualities are most important?" |
| **Code** column | Tags each row against Stage 1: *Transfer and Skill Goals* / *Meaning and Knowledge Goals*, plus the audit question "Are all desired results being appropriately assessed?" |
| **Rubric(s)** | Criterion-based scoring tools derived from the Evaluative Criteria. Practitioner convention (Wiggins & McTighe) uses four criterion types: **impact** (was the desired result achieved), **content** (proficiency, accuracy, thoroughness), **quality** (craftsmanship, mechanics, attention to detail), **process** (how the work was accomplished) |
| **Self-assessment/reflection prompt** | Template 1.0 asks explicitly: "How will students reflect upon and self-assess their learning?" |

The Code column is the **alignment matrix in embryo** — it is literally a per-evidence-row traceability link back to Stage 1. In higher-ed practice this is externalized as a *course alignment map* / *Course Design Matrix* (outcome × assessment × activity × material), which several centers require before content development begins ([Salisbury ID&D](https://www.salisbury.edu/administration/academic-affairs/instructional-design-delivery/articles/backward-design.aspx); [K. Patricia Cross Academy](https://kpcrossacademy.ua.edu/beyond-learning-objectives-backward-design-as-a-framework-for-instructional-alignment/)).

### GRASPS — the performance-task authoring schema

*Nutshell*, Stage 2 note 5: "GRASPS is an acronym to help designers construct authentic scenarios for performance tasks."

| Letter | Field | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **G** | Goal | The goal or challenge statement in the scenario |
| **R** | Role | The role the student plays |
| **A** | Audience | The audience/client the student must satisfy |
| **S** | Situation | The setting/context, with its constraints and opportunities |
| **P** | Performance | The specific performance or product expected |
| **S** | Standards | The criteria the product/performance will be judged against (the second S; in the *Nutshell*'s condensed list this collapses into the adjacent bullet "evaluated using valid criteria and indicators … related to the Desired Results of Stage 1") |

Task requirements per the *Nutshell*: performance tasks must reflect the six facets; "establish real-world contexts, demands, **messiness**, audiences, and purposes"; "should be written in the GRASPS format"; and be "evaluated using valid criteria and indicators, reflective of not only quality performance but related to the Desired Results of Stage 1."

Note the role of **Other Evidence** beyond coverage: it "can overlap the performance-based evidence, thereby increasing the reliability of the overall assessment (especially if the performance task was done by a group)." That is a psychometric argument, not a bookkeeping one — group performance tasks under-determine individual attainment, and Other Evidence disambiguates.

### Quality gate — UbD Design Standards, Stage 2

"To what extent do the assessments provide **fair, valid, reliable and sufficient measures of the desired results**?" Consider — are:

- students asked to exhibit their understanding through **"authentic" performance tasks**?
- **appropriate criterion-based scoring tools** used to evaluate student products and performances?
- a **variety** of appropriate assessment formats providing additional evidence of learning?

The white paper adds the alignment audit for this stage: "are we assessing everything that we are trying to achieve (in Stage 1), or only those things that are easiest to test and grade? Is anything important slipping through the cracks because it is not being assessed?"

### Stage 2 failure modes

- Assessing only what is convenient to grade — the "easiest to test and grade" trap named above. Detected by an empty cell in the Code column for a Stage 1 transfer or meaning goal.
- Performance tasks that are elaborate *activities* rather than evidence — the activity-oriented sin migrating into Stage 2. The Prairie Day example in Wiggins's *Overview* is the canonical illustration: the "before" unit assesses "completion of seven learning stations," which measures compliance, not understanding.
- Contexts that are sanitized rather than messy, or role/audience that is nominal ("write a report for your teacher") — this collapses GRASPS back into a school exercise.
- Rubric criteria that score only *quality* (neatness, mechanics) and never *impact* — the task then cannot distinguish a polished miss from a rough success.
- Six-facet checklist-ism: forcing all six facets into one task.

## 3. Stage 3 — Learning Plan

### Driving questions

*Nutshell*:

- What activities, experiences, and lessons will lead to achievement of the desired results and success at the assessments?
- How will the learning plan help students with Acquisition, Meaning Making, and Transfer?
- How will the unit be sequenced and differentiated to optimize achievement for all learners?

### Inputs

Stage 1 + Stage 2 artifacts. Content, texts, and resources are selected **here, last** — this is the most-cited operational consequence of backward design in higher-ed practice ([University of Iowa](https://teaching.center.uiowa.edu/designing-intentional-courses-through-backward-design): instructional materials and content are selected last, after objectives and assessments are established).

*Nutshell*, Stage 3: teaching "should employ resources most appropriate to the goals (not simply march through a textbook or commercial program)" and "should reflect the instructional approaches most appropriate to the goals (not what is easiest or most comfortable for the teacher)."

### Artifacts produced

**Template 2.0, Stage 3 — Learning Plan** fields:

| Field | Prompt |
|---|---|
| **Pre-Assessment** (full-width band at top) | "What pre-assessments will you use to check students' prior knowledge, skill levels, and potential misconceptions?" |
| **Code** (left column) | "What's the goal for (or type of) each learning event?" — each event tagged **A**, **M**, **T** (Acquisition / Meaning / Transfer), often plus WHERETO letters. Example from the template: `1. Hook T, M` / `2. M, A` / `3. T, M` / `4. M, A` / `5. A, T` / `6. M, T` |
| **Learning Events** (center) | Numbered, sequenced events. Framing line: "Student success at transfer, meaning, and acquisition depends upon …" |
| **Progress Monitoring** (right column) | Three sub-prompts: "How will you monitor students' progress toward acquisition, meaning, and transfer, during lesson events?" · "What are potential rough spots and student misunderstandings?" · "How will students get the feedback they need?" |
| **Embedded self-check** (bottom of Learning Events) | "Are all three types of goals (acquisition, meaning, and transfer) addressed in the learning plan?" · "Does the learning plan reflect principles of learning and best practices?" · "Is there tight alignment with Stages 1 and 2?" · "Is the plan likely to be engaging and effective for all students?" |

The **A/M/T coding is the load-bearing mechanism of Stage 3.** White paper, Stage 3: "We suggest that teachers code the various events in their learning plan with the letters T, M, and A to ensure that all three goals are addressed in instruction. Too often, teaching focuses primarily on presenting information or modeling basic skills for acquisition without extending the lessons to help students make meaning or transfer the learning."

### WHERETO — the learning-plan design and self-audit heuristic

*Nutshell*, Stage 3 note 4: "WHERETO is an acronym for considering **and self-assessing** the key elements and logic of a learning plan." Note it is a **checklist over the plan, not a sequence** — the letters are not the order of instruction.

| Letter | Element | Operational meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **W** | Where / Why / What | Student sees the big picture, has answers to "Why?", and knows the final performance expectations and evaluative criteria as early as possible. (Template 1.0 adds the reciprocal: help the *teacher* know where students are coming from — prior knowledge, interests.) |
| **H** | Hook & Hold | Immerse the student immediately in the ideas and issues; thought-provoking experiences/challenges/questions at the heart of the unit |
| **E** | Equip & Experience | Provide tools, resources, skill, and information needed to reach the understandings and succeed at the performance tasks |
| **R** | Rethink, Rehearse, Revise, Refine | Shift perspective, consider alternative theories, challenge prior assumptions, introduce new evidence; and give the impetus and opportunity to revise and polish prior work |
| **E** | Evaluate | Diagnostic and formative feedback; opportunities to self-assess and self-adjust |
| **T** | Tailor | Differentiate instruction, assignments, assessments to readiness/interest/preference — "without sacrificing validity or rigor" |
| **O** | Organize | Sequence the work to suit the understanding goals — explicitly "questioning the flow provided by the textbook, which is typically organized around discrete topics" |

### Quality gate — UbD Design Standards, Stage 3

"To what extent is the learning plan **effective and engaging**?" Consider — will the students:

- know **where** they're going (the learning goals), **why**, and **what** is required of them (performance requirements and evaluative criteria)?
- be **hooked** — engaged in digging into the big ideas through inquiry, research, problem-solving, experimentation?
- have adequate opportunities to **explore/experience** big ideas and receive instruction to **equip** them for the required performance(s)?
- have sufficient opportunities to **rethink, rehearse, revise and/or refine** their work based upon timely feedback?
- have an opportunity to **self-evaluate** their work, reflect on their learning, and set future goals?

And: is the plan **tailored** and flexible to address all students, and **organized** and sequenced to maximize engagement and effectiveness?

The Stage 3 standard is WHERETO restated as review criteria. This is deliberate: the same heuristic authors and audits the artifact.

### Stage 3 failure modes

- All-A plans: every learning event coded A, none coded M or T. Named directly in the white paper. This is the most mechanically detectable defect in the entire framework.
- Understandings delivered by telling. *Nutshell* Stage 3 note 3: "Understandings cannot be simply told; they have to be actively 'constructed' by the learner." A learning plan whose meaning-making event is a lecture is self-defeating.
- No **R** — the plan is a single pass with no revision cycle, so the performance task is a one-shot exam in disguise.
- **W** deferred — students see the rubric and task the week it is due. The Prairie Day "after" example fixes this explicitly: "Review the scoring rubrics for 'memory box,' museum display, letters, and journals **before** students begin the performance tasks."
- Textbook order used as unit order (violates **O**).
- Differentiation that changes the construct being measured rather than the route to it (the "without sacrificing validity or rigor" clause).

## 4. Overall alignment gate and the peer-review ritual

**UbD Design Standards, Overall Design:** "To what extent is the entire unit **coherent, with the elements of all 3 stages aligned**?"

Alignment is defined in the white paper as the requirement that "all three stages must clearly align not only to standards, but also to one another. In other words, the Stage 1 content and understanding must be what is assessed in Stage 2 and taught in Stage 3."

The review is a **social process, not a solo checklist**. *Nutshell* tenet 7: "Regular reviews of curriculum against design standards enhance curricular quality and effectiveness." White paper tenet 6 adds that this "provides engaging and professional discussions." Standard practice: designers exchange draft units and act as peer reviewers using the Design Standards, giving structured feedback and suggested edits. The Design Standards document is written as consider-questions, not as a numeric rubric — the output of a review is *commentary and revision suggestions*, not a score.

**Post-implementation loop.** *Nutshell* tenet 8: "UbD reflects a 'continuous improvement' approach. The results of curriculum designs — student performance — informs needed adjustments." The unit is a hypothesis; student work is the test. Stage 2 evidence, once collected from real students, re-enters Stage 1 (were these the right priorities?) and Stage 3 (where did students actually stall?).

## 5. Where unstructured source material enters

There is no single "research phase" in UbD. Raw source material enters at four distinct points with four distinct transformations:

| Entry point | Source material | Transformation |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1, goals** | Standards documents, program outcomes, accreditation criteria, syllabi, competency frameworks, course catalogs | **Unpacking** — verbatim requirement text → transfer goals, understandings, EQs, knowledge, skill. Tenet 3: UbD "unpacks and transforms Content Standards into the relevant Stage 1 elements." Not extraction; inference. |
| **Stage 1, big ideas** | The discipline itself — scholarly literature, primary sources, expert/SME interviews, professional practice | **Distillation to generalizations.** The designer must identify what is "at the heart of the discipline" and "in need of uncoverage." This requires domain judgment that no standards document supplies, and is where SME input is irreplaceable. |
| **Stage 2, authentic contexts** | Real-world professional scenarios, case material, current events, client/industry problems, data sets | **Scenario construction** via GRASPS. The task's Role/Audience/Situation are drawn from how the discipline is actually practiced — the *Nutshell* demands "real-world contexts, demands, messiness, audiences, and purposes." Fabricated-feeling scenarios are the failure signature. |
| **Stage 3, resources** | Textbooks, readings, media, datasets, tools, simulations, non-fiction at varied reading levels | **Selection against goals**, deliberately last. The Prairie Day "after" example shows this concretely: substituting an Oregon Trail simulation for a "dress the pioneer" game, and adding "non-fiction sources to accommodate various reading levels." |

The important asymmetry: material entering at Stage 1 is *interrogated for ideas*; material entering at Stage 3 is *selected as means*. The same textbook can be both, but it must never be allowed to determine Stage 1 — that is precisely the coverage sin.

## 6. Iterative vs. linear

The stage **logic** is strictly ordered; the design **process** is not. This distinction is explicit in the UbD literature as the *nonlinear design process*: the final product must obey the three-stage logic, while the act of designing typically unfolds unpredictably.

Concretely:

- **Forward dependencies are real.** Stage 2 cannot be validated without Stage 1; Stage 3 cannot be validated without Stage 2. The Code columns in Template 2.0 exist to make these dependencies checkable.
- **Backward edits are routine.** *Nutshell* Stage 1 note 7 requires knowing what you will assess (Stage 2) and address (Stage 3) before finalizing the Stage 1 knowledge/skill list. Drafting a GRASPS task frequently reveals that a stated understanding is untestable or that an EQ is really a comprehension question — forcing a Stage 1 rewrite.
- **Entry points vary.** Practitioners commonly start from an existing favorite activity or an existing assessment and work outward to reconstruct Stage 1, then prune. Wiggins's *Overview* is built entirely around this: two "before & after" units (3rd-grade Prairie Day; 10th-grade surface area and volume) where existing activity- and coverage-driven units are retrofitted through backward design. The "after" units keep some original activities (Exploration 22, the memory box) but re-justify each against newly articulated goals and drop or replace those that cannot be justified.
- **The Stage 1 → Stage 3 shortcut is the sin.** Whatever the entry point, skipping Stage 2 is what produces the twin sins. Stage 2 is the load-bearing stage precisely because it is the one people omit.
- **The unit-level loop closes after delivery** (tenet 8), making the whole thing a design-implement-observe-revise cycle rather than a one-shot artifact.

Practical consequence for automation: the pipeline needs **explicit revision edges** from Stage 2 → Stage 1 and Stage 3 → Stage 2/1, and a convergence criterion (the Overall Design alignment standard) rather than a fixed pass count.

## 7. Higher-education adaptation (what differs from K-12 UbD)

Most current higher-ed centers for teaching and learning use backward design in a somewhat lighter form. Common deviations, drawn from the institutional sources above:

- **Vocabulary shift.** "Established goals" become *course learning outcomes* / *program learning outcomes*; measurability (often Bloom-verb-checked, sometimes SMART/A-SMART-formatted) is emphasized more than in the original UbD, which resists reducing understandings to behavioral objectives.
- **A situational-analysis step is prepended.** Iowa's five-step version begins with analysis of course structure, subject matter, student demographics, institutional expectations, and context, then goals, then assessment, then activities, then an explicit *integration and realignment* pass. This is Fink's *Integrated Course Design* blended into UbD — the "Prioritizing Framework Tool" and Fink's six dimensions (foundational knowledge, application, integration, human dimension, caring, learning how to learn) commonly stand in for the Transfer/Meaning/Acquisition tiers.
- **The alignment map is the deliverable.** Where K-12 UbD hands off a completed template, higher-ed ID practice frequently produces a *course design matrix* / *alignment table* mapping outcome → assessment → activity → material, reviewed with the SME before content build. Salisbury explicitly instructs faculty to work with an instructional designer using a provided Course Design Matrix.
- **Enduring understandings and essential questions are often dropped**, collapsing Stage 1 to outcomes only. This is the most common lossy adaptation and it re-admits the coverage sin, since outcomes lists have no mechanism for prioritizing big ideas.
- **A two-role workflow.** The designer (ID) owns process and artifact quality; the SME owns disciplinary truth and big-idea identification. Review gates sit at the boundary between them.

## 8. Mechanization Notes

Modeling backward design as a multi-agent pipeline. Artifact names below are proposed as stable identifiers for a spec. Every stage emits a typed artifact; every stage is gated.

### Cross-cutting design decisions

- **Artifact store, not conversation.** Each stage reads and writes named artifacts. Downstream stages must reference upstream artifacts **by ID**, not restate them, so alignment is checkable mechanically.
- **Traceability is first-class.** Template 2.0's Code columns are already an alignment matrix. Model them as an explicit `AlignmentMatrix` with edges `evidence_item → stage1_goal` and `learning_event → {A|M|T} × stage1_goal`. Most quality checks reduce to graph queries over this: *unassessed Stage 1 goal* = Stage 1 node with no incoming Stage 2 edge; *orphan activity* = learning event with no outgoing edge; *all-A plan* = no learning event tagged M or T.
- **Two agent kinds per stage.** A *generator* and a *critic*. The critic runs the corresponding UbD Design Standard as its rubric and returns findings, not a score — matching how human peer review actually works.
- **Revision edges, not a straight line.** Allow Stage 2 → Stage 1 and Stage 3 → {Stage 2, Stage 1} amendment proposals. Cap iterations; converge on the Overall Design gate.
- **Model choice.** Stage 1 big-idea identification and Stage 2 authenticity judgment are the two steps where model quality dominates; Stage 3 assembly is comparatively mechanical.
- **Where automation is weakest:** deciding what is genuinely "in need of uncoverage," judging whether a GRASPS scenario reads as authentic to a practitioner, and setting rigor level for a specific cohort. All three are SME-judgment calls and should be human gates, not model gates.

### Step 0 — Source Ingestion & Situational Analysis

- **Inputs:** unstructured corpus (standards/outcomes documents, syllabi, competency frameworks, textbooks, primary sources, SME notes/transcripts); context parameters (audience, prior knowledge, duration, modality, constraints).
- **Outputs:**
  - `SourceCorpusIndex` — chunked, cited, provenance-tagged source material
  - `EstablishedGoals[]` — verbatim external requirements with citations to source
  - `ContextProfile` — learner audience, prerequisites, time budget, modality, institutional constraints
  - `DomainConceptMap` — candidate big ideas, recurring concepts, expert disagreements, known misconceptions, extracted from the corpus
- **Judgment required:** distinguishing requirement text (must be preserved verbatim) from disciplinary content (must be interpreted). Deciding corpus sufficiency.
- **Human review before proceeding:** Is the corpus the right corpus? Are the established goals complete and correctly scoped to this unit? Is the time budget realistic? Cheap to fix here, expensive later.

### Step 1 — Stage 1: Desired Results

- **Inputs:** `EstablishedGoals[]`, `ContextProfile`, `DomainConceptMap`, `SourceCorpusIndex`.
- **Outputs:**
  - `TransferGoals[]` — stem-constrained: "Students will be able to independently use their learning to …"
  - `EnduringUnderstandings[]` — stem-constrained: "Students will understand that …", each required to be a *specific generalization*, each linked to ≥1 `TransferGoal`
  - `EssentialQuestions[]` — stem "Students will keep considering …", each paired to ≥1 understanding
  - `KnowledgeItems[]` — "Students will know …"
  - `SkillItems[]` — "Students will be skilled at …"
  - `PredictedMisconceptions[]` — feeds Step 3's Progress Monitoring
  - `Stage1Traceability` — each artifact → source citation and/or "inferred, not in source" flag
- **Judgment required:** the hardest step. Deciding what is enduring vs. merely important; framing generalizations that are non-obvious and contestable; authoring transfer goals that the standards do not state (*Nutshell* Stage 1 note 5 requires this); ruthless pruning under note 7. An automated generator will over-produce — expect to generate 15 candidate understandings and keep 3.
- **Automatable checks (critic agent):** stem conformance; understandings that are noun phrases rather than propositions; EQs answerable with a fact (yes/no or single-fact detection); duplicate/near-duplicate understandings; understandings with no linked EQ; knowledge/skill list length exceeding a budget derived from `ContextProfile`.
- **Not automatable:** "in need of uncoverage." A model will confidently rate its own platitudes as profound.
- **Human review before proceeding:** **This is the most important gate in the pipeline.** An SME must confirm the enduring understandings are true, non-trivial, and central to the discipline, and that the transfer goals describe something a practitioner actually does. Everything downstream inherits errors here and no later gate can catch them. Also confirm the prune: what was dropped and why.

### Step 2 — Stage 2: Evidence

- **Inputs:** all Stage 1 artifacts; `ContextProfile`; the Six Facets as a selector; authentic-context material from `SourceCorpusIndex`.
- **Outputs:**
  - `FacetSelection` — per understanding, which facet(s) yield appropriate evidence, with rationale (must justify *exclusions* too)
  - `PerformanceTasks[]` — each a structured `GRASPS` record: `{goal, role, audience, situation, performance, standards}` plus `facets[]`, `linked_goals[]`, `deliverable_spec`
  - `OtherEvidence[]` — quizzes, prompts, observations, work samples, each with `linked_goals[]`
  - `EvaluativeCriteria[]` — per assessment, tagged by criterion type `{impact, content, quality, process}`
  - `Rubrics[]` — criterion × performance-level matrices derived from `EvaluativeCriteria`
  - `SelfAssessmentPrompts[]`
  - `AlignmentMatrix.stage2` — the Code column, materialized: every Stage 1 goal → covering evidence item(s)
- **Judgment required:** whether a scenario is genuinely authentic and appropriately *messy* rather than a school exercise in costume; whether the evidence is sufficient and fair; whether group-task evidence needs individual corroboration (the *Nutshell*'s reliability point); what rigor level fits the cohort.
- **Automatable checks (critic agent):** coverage — every `TransferGoal` and `EnduringUnderstanding` has ≥1 linked evidence item, and transfer goals are covered by a *performance task* specifically, not merely by a quiz; GRASPS completeness (all six fields non-empty and non-generic); rubric criterion-type coverage, specifically flagging rubrics with no `impact` criterion; facet over-use (all six facets on one task); format variety in `OtherEvidence`.
- **Human review before proceeding:** SME judges authenticity of each GRASPS scenario ("would a professional recognize this situation?") and appropriateness of rigor; reviews the coverage report for goals that are formally covered but weakly covered. Also: confirm no important goal was quietly dropped from Stage 1 to make Stage 2 easier.
- **Revision edge:** Stage 2 commonly emits `Stage1AmendmentProposals[]` — untestable understandings, EQs that turned out to be comprehension questions, missing enabling skills discovered while specifying the task. Route back to Step 1.

### Step 3 — Stage 3: Learning Plan

- **Inputs:** all Stage 1 and Stage 2 artifacts; `PredictedMisconceptions[]`; candidate resources from `SourceCorpusIndex`; `ContextProfile` (time budget is binding here).
- **Outputs:**
  - `PreAssessments[]` — targeting prior knowledge, skill level, and specifically the `PredictedMisconceptions[]`
  - `LearningEvents[]` — ordered; each `{id, description, amt_codes[], whereto_codes[], linked_goals[], linked_assessments[], resources[], duration}`
  - `ResourceSelections[]` — chosen *after* events are specified, each justified against a goal; this ordering must be enforced by the pipeline, not left to the model
  - `ProgressMonitoringPlan` — per-event or per-phase: monitoring method, anticipated rough spots, feedback mechanism
  - `DifferentiationPlan` — the **T** in WHERETO, with an explicit note on which adaptations change route vs. construct
  - `AlignmentMatrix.stage3` — every learning event → Stage 1 goal(s) and A/M/T type
- **Judgment required:** sequencing for understanding rather than for topic order (**O**); pacing against the real time budget; choosing instructional approaches suited to goals rather than to convenience; designing revision cycles (**R**) that actually fit the calendar.
- **Automatable checks (critic agent):** A/M/T distribution — flag if no events coded M or T (the single most valuable automated check in the framework); WHERETO coverage — flag any letter with zero events, especially **R** and **E**(valuate); **W** timing — flag if the rubric/task reveal event is not near the front of the sequence; orphan events with no `linked_goals`; unprepared performance tasks — a `PerformanceTask` whose required `SkillItems` are never equipped by an **E** event; duration sum vs. time budget; resources not traceable to a goal.
- **Human review before proceeding:** an instructor reviews feasibility — pacing, resource availability, whether the revision cycle is real, whether differentiation preserves rigor. Feasibility is the thing models are worst at and instructors are best at.
- **Revision edge:** Stage 3 emits `Stage2AmendmentProposals[]` (task too large for the calendar; missing formative checkpoint) and occasionally `Stage1AmendmentProposals[]` (scope exceeds available time → prune goals).

### Step 4 — Design Review Gate (Overall Coherence)

- **Inputs:** the complete `UnitDesign` artifact set + `AlignmentMatrix`.
- **Outputs:**
  - `DesignStandardsReview` — findings against all four UbD Design Standards (Stage 1 big ideas · Stage 2 valid/fair/reliable/sufficient evidence · Stage 3 effective and engaging · Overall coherence), each as `{standard, finding, severity, affected_artifact_ids, suggested_edit}`
  - `CoherenceReport` — graph-derived: unassessed goals, untaught assessed content, orphan events, A/M/T imbalance, WHERETO gaps
  - `RevisionQueue` — prioritized edits routed back to the owning stage
- **Judgment required:** whether findings are blocking or advisory; when to stop iterating. Human peer review in real practice produces commentary and suggested edits, not a pass/fail — model the same way.
- **Design note:** implement this as an **independent critic that has not seen the generation trajectory**, mirroring the peer-review ritual. A critic that co-authored the unit will ratify it.
- **Human review before proceeding:** sign-off on the assembled unit; adjudicate any finding the pipeline flagged as blocking; explicitly approve the twin-sins check (is this a set of activities in UbD clothing?).

### Step 5 — Delivery Feedback Loop (out of scope for v1, but the loop exists)

- **Inputs:** actual student work against `PerformanceTasks[]` and `OtherEvidence[]`; `Rubrics[]` scores; observed rough spots.
- **Outputs:** `ImplementationEvidence`, `DesignAdjustments[]` routed to Stages 1–3.
- **Judgment required:** distinguishing a design defect from an execution defect from a cohort variation.
- **Human review:** entirely human — this is instructor observation, and the pipeline should ingest it as a first-class input rather than infer it.

### Summary — stage → gate → owner

| Step | Emits | Automated gate | Human gate | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Ingestion | `SourceCorpusIndex`, `EstablishedGoals`, `ContextProfile`, `DomainConceptMap` | provenance completeness | corpus sufficiency, scope | SME + designer |
| 1 Desired Results | `TransferGoals`, `EnduringUnderstandings`, `EssentialQuestions`, `Knowledge/SkillItems`, `PredictedMisconceptions` | stem conformance, EQ openness, list budget | **truth & centrality of understandings (critical)** | SME |
| 2 Evidence | `PerformanceTasks (GRASPS)`, `OtherEvidence`, `EvaluativeCriteria`, `Rubrics`, `AlignmentMatrix.stage2` | goal coverage, GRASPS completeness, rubric impact-criterion | authenticity & rigor of scenarios | SME |
| 3 Learning Plan | `PreAssessments`, `LearningEvents`, `ResourceSelections`, `ProgressMonitoringPlan`, `DifferentiationPlan` | A/M/T balance, WHERETO coverage, time budget, orphan events | feasibility & pacing | instructor |
| 4 Review | `DesignStandardsReview`, `CoherenceReport`, `RevisionQueue` | graph coherence queries | blocking/advisory adjudication, twin-sins check | peer reviewer |
| 5 Delivery | `ImplementationEvidence`, `DesignAdjustments` | — | all | instructor |

### Highest-value automation, ranked

1. **`AlignmentMatrix` maintenance and coherence queries.** Purely mechanical, catches the defects practitioners most often miss, and is exactly what the Code columns were invented to do by hand.
2. **A/M/T balance check on Stage 3.** One query; catches the failure the white paper singles out as most common.
3. **GRASPS field completion.** Highly structured schema, well-suited to generation, cheap to review.
4. **Candidate over-generation with human pruning at Stage 1.** Plays to model strengths (breadth) and human strengths (judging depth).

### Lowest-value / highest-risk automation

- Autonomous Stage 1 sign-off. A model will produce fluent, plausible "enduring understandings" that are platitudes, and will rate them highly. The Design Standard's phrase *"in need of uncoverage"* has no automatable proxy.
- Authenticity scoring of GRASPS scenarios without a practitioner in the loop.
- Auto-approving differentiation, which can silently change the construct being assessed.
