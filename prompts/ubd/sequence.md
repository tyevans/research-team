This is the **O** of WHERETO, taken on its own: organise and sequence the work
so that understanding is built rather than covered.

The events already exist. You are not designing new ones, not merging them, not
rewriting their purpose, and not adding to the intents or the evidence. You are
deciding what order they happen in, and then showing that the order works.

That sounds like the small job of the three stages and it is not, because
sequence is where a plan that reads well fails in practice. Two units with the
same events and different orders are different units.

## What the order must satisfy

**Order for understanding, not for topic.** The default order is whatever the
source material used, and the source material was almost certainly organised by
topic — which is how reference works are organised, and reference works are not
curricula. Question that flow explicitly. The order should follow how the
understandings get built: a case before the generalisation drawn from it, the
contradiction before the resolution, the failed intuition before the correction.

**Where the unit is going comes first.** Learners must know early what they are
working toward, why it matters, and what they will be judged by. This is not a
courtesy; a learner who does not know the destination cannot tell which parts of
the journey matter, and every subsequent event costs more than it should. Put
the orienting events, and the reveal of the performance task and its criteria,
in the opening quarter of the sequence.

**Nothing may be needed before it is equipped.** Each performance task
presupposes skills and knowledge; the events that equip them must come before
the task requires them. This is the failure that survives review most reliably,
because a task that requires a skill taught two weeks later reads perfectly on
the page and only fails in a room. Walk the requirements of each task against
the positions of the events that meet them, one at a time, and place the tasks
accordingly.

Where a requirement is met by nothing at all, say so plainly in your prose
rather than choosing an order that hides it. An unmet prerequisite is a real
finding about the design and the reviewer needs it.

**Revision needs room after feedback and before the work counts.** A revision
opportunity placed immediately after the thing it revises, with no feedback in
between, is a second attempt rather than a rethink. The rethink events need
something new to think with — evidence, a perspective, a contrary case — and the
sequence is what supplies it.

**Meaning-making cannot all be at the end.** A sequence that front-loads every
acquisition event and postpones meaning and transfer to the final week is an
all-acquisition unit with a bolted-on ending. Interleave: learners should have
something to make meaning of early and should return to it with more each time.

## The alignment record

Settle the intent-by-experience matrix: every intent against the events that
serve it, in the order they now occur.

Read it twice, in both directions. An intent whose row is empty is taught by
nothing — a promise the unit does not keep. An event whose column is empty
serves nothing, and an event serving nothing is an activity, which is the first
of the two sins this methodology exists to prevent. Neither of those is
detectable by reading the plan straight through, which is why the matrix is
built rather than described.

An intent served only by events clustered in one week is covered on paper and
not in fact: understanding needs return visits. The matrix shows that as a
cluster, and it is worth reporting even though it is not a gap.

## What the opening blocks carry

The ordering carries a `position` for every event, as consecutive numbers, and
the events keep the identities the plan gave them — the order is a new fact
about existing events, not a new set of events.

`links` on the ordering names the plan and the intents it is an order over.

Where your ordering forced a compromise — a prerequisite met only just in time,
a revision cycle tighter than it should be, a task that had to move because
nothing equipped it earlier — write it down. The compromises are the part of a
sequence a reviewer can actually act on.
